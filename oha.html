Operational Health Assessment

This is not a quiz and not a report card. It produces a work order: one scored number, eight scored areas, and a single indicated place to intervene first.

Who completes it
The owner-operator. If you have a manager or second-in-command, have them complete a separate copy independently.
Time to complete
8–12 minutes. Do not deliberate. First-instinct scores are more accurate than considered ones.
Timeframe
Rate each item based on the last 90 days.
Retest
90 days after intervention begins. Same instrument, same rater, no reference to prior scores until after completion.

Scoring key

Rate each item on the following scale. Two things are being measured at once: how often it happens, and how much it costs you when it does.

0
Never or almost never occurs
1
Occasionally occurs — impact is not severe
2
Occasionally occurs — impact is severe
3
Frequently occurs — impact is not severe
4
Frequently occurs — impact is severe

The treatment target

Two rules override the total. The Bottleneck Rule: any single section scoring 16 or higher is the treatment target regardless of total burden — one area, not all eight. The Capacity Rule: if Owner Capacity scores 20 or higher it is addressed first no matter what else scored higher, because an operator in that range cannot execute any downstream intervention.

Use notes

  1. The instrument produces the work order, not a report card. Score → identify the bottleneck → intervene at that area only → retest at 90 days. A section that has not moved by retest means the intervention was wrong, not that more time is needed.
  2. Compare owner and manager copies before discussing results. A spread of more than 8 points on any section means the leadership team is not seeing the same business. That finding usually outranks whatever the scores themselves say.
  3. Score before the discovery conversation, not during it. Owners revise their answers in real time when a consultant is watching.
  4. Retest data is the proof asset. Three clients with documented pre/post burden reductions is more persuasive than any case study written in prose.
